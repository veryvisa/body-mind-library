Step 2 · The main line

In one sentence

Core idea

Assertive communication means standing up for yourself calmly while respecting others, and it tends to bring better results than staying silent or lashing out.

What the author is getting at

Assertiveness respects your rights and other people's rights at once. Passive and aggressive reactions both leave you feeling worse, while an assertive one makes success and self-respect more likely.

Step 5 · Four angles

Look at it from four sides

What the book says

Assertiveness respects your rights and other people's rights at once. Passive and aggressive reactions both leave you feeling worse, while an assertive one makes success and self-respect more likely.

Common mix-up

A common misreading

'Being assertive means being pushy.' Not quite: pushy is aggressive. Assertive is calm and fair to both sides. It can still feel hard at first, and a counselor or other adult can help you find the words. If a situation feels unsafe, seek an adult rather than facing it alone.

Does it still hold today?

What research says (open when you're curious)

Direct evidence for teens is thin. A systematic review and meta-analysis of nine trials with nurses and nursing students found that assertive communication training improved speaking-up behavior (SMD 0.58) (Chen et al., 2023). That is an adult workplace setting, so it only shows that assertiveness can be taught, not that it raises teen self-esteem.

  • Chen HW, Wu JC, Kang YN, Chiu YJ, Hu SH. Assertive communication training for nurses to speak up in cases of medical errors: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Nurse Educ Today 2023;126():105831. link
