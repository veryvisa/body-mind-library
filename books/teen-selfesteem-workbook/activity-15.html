Step 2 · The main line

In one sentence

Core idea

Many beliefs are handed to you; looking at each one lets you keep what is truly yours and rewrite what is not.

What the author is getting at

Your beliefs shape your thoughts, feelings, and actions, and some may not reflect your authentic self. Looking at them helps you understand what is important to you.

What is a belief?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
An idea you hold about the world, life, right and wrong, or good and bad. It affects how you think, feel, and act.
Activity 15 · Discovering Your Beliefs
Where do beliefs come from?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
Mostly from family, friends, culture, faith, and what you learned growing up. Some you agree with, and some you simply never questioned.
Activity 15 · Discovering Your Beliefs
Can you have a belief that others disagree with?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
Yes. You have a right to your beliefs. They can match or differ from family and friends, and it is fine to be unsure.
Activity 15 · Discovering Your Beliefs
What does rewriting a belief mean?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
Changing the wording of a belief you disagree with so it fits what you actually think now.

Step 5 · Four angles

Look at it from four sides

What the book says

Your beliefs shape your thoughts, feelings, and actions, and some may not reflect your authentic self. Looking at them helps you understand what is important to you.

Common mix-up

A common misreading

"If I question a belief from my family, I am disrespecting them." Questioning is a way to understand it. You can keep a belief because you checked it and agree, and many big topics are sensitive, so talk them over with a trusted adult if that helps.

Does it still hold today?

What research says (open when you're curious)

One well-known field experiment had students write briefly about values that mattered to them. For African American students in the study, it was linked to better grades and a roughly 40 percent smaller achievement gap (Cohen et al., 2006). The finding is specific to that school setting and group, and later results vary, so it supports the idea that connecting to your own values can help, not that a writing task fixes things.

  • Cohen GL, Garcia J, Apfel N, Master A. Reducing the racial achievement gap: a social-psychological intervention. Science 2006;313(5791). link

How it connects

It follows Activity 14 and leads to Activity 16 (passions). Beliefs about yourself you learned from others link to Activity 4 (devaluing messages) and Activity 5 (positive self-messages); later, Activity 39 (conviction) is about acting on beliefs.
