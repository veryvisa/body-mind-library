Step 2 · The main line

In one sentence

Core idea

Social media has real benefits, but comparing, cyberbullying and fear of missing out can wear down self-esteem, so notice your own patterns and use small tools to stay in charge.

What the author is getting at

Social media is neither good nor bad by itself; noticing how it affects you and using simple tools lets you keep its benefits without losing your footing.

What is FOMO, and what is JOMO?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
FOMO is the worry that others are having a better time without you. JOMO is the joy of missing out: enjoying what you are doing right now.

Step 5 · Four angles

Look at it from four sides

What the book says

Social media is neither good nor bad by itself; noticing how it affects you and using simple tools lets you keep its benefits without losing your footing.

Common mix-up

A common misreading

'If social media makes me feel bad, it is my fault, or I must quit completely.' The activity points to a middle path: notice your pattern, keep your privacy settings tight, and use limits and breaks. If you are being bullied or feel very low, tell a trusted adult or counselor.

Does it still hold today?

What research says (open when you're curious)

A study of about 1,000 adults found that passively using Facebook, meaning scrolling without interacting, was linked to lower well-being through upward comparison, while actively using Instagram was linked to more life satisfaction through social support (Masciantonio et al., 2021). A 2026 scoping review of studies of adolescents cautions that time spent alone is a blunt measure and that what teens do online matters (Sursely et al., 2026). Most of this evidence is correlational, so it points to patterns and does not prove that social media alone causes mood changes.

  • Masciantonio A, Bourguignon D, Bouchat P, et al. Don't put all social network sites in one basket: Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, TikTok, and their relations with well-being during the COVID-19 pandemic. PLoS One 2021;16(3):e0248384. link
  • Sursely J, Baran M, Bardhoshi G, et al. Time out: a scoping review of non-duration based social media use measures and adolescent mental health. J Adolesc 2026. link
