Step 2 · The main line

In one sentence

Core idea

Daydreams, night dreams, and wishes for the future hint at what you value and long for, even when they are hazy or conflicted.

What the author is getting at

Daydreams and goals are clues to who you really are and what you want. Following them with curiosity teaches you about your authentic self.

Step 5 · Four angles

Look at it from four sides

What the book says

Daydreams and goals are clues to who you really are and what you want. Following them with curiosity teaches you about your authentic self.

Common mix-up

A common misreading

"Dreams must turn into plans, or they are a waste." For this activity, dreams are information about you first, not promises. Planning comes later (see Activity 43), and a dream can change without being a failure.

Does it still hold today?

What research says (open when you're curious)

Researchers who compared two ways of thinking about the future found that expecting a good outcome to be likely went with more effort and better results, but enjoying positive fantasies about it did not, and sometimes went with less effort (Oettingen & Mayer, 2002; PubMed record year 2003). Findings come from job seekers, students, and patients, not teens specifically. A fair reading is that dreams are useful for discovering what you value, and later pairing them with realistic steps may help, which is where Activity 43 comes in.

  • Oettingen G, Mayer D. The motivating function of thinking about the future: expectations versus fantasies. J Pers Soc Psychol 2002;83(5). link

How it connects

It builds on Activity 13 and sits beside Activity 15 (beliefs) and Activity 16 (passions). Turning dreams into steps is Activity 43 (realistic goals); the calm breathing here echoes Activity 34 (inner peace).
