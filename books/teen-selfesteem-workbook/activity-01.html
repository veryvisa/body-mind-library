Step 2 · The main line

In one sentence

Core idea

Healthy self-esteem is a quiet, steady feeling that you and everyone else matter equally, so you can own your flaws and enjoy your strengths without putting anyone down.

What the author is getting at

Self-esteem is your inner sense of worth. Healthy self-esteem lets you accept faults, enjoy strengths, respect others, and not depend on wins or praise.

Step 5 · Four angles

Look at it from four sides

What the book says

Self-esteem is your inner sense of worth. Healthy self-esteem lets you accept faults, enjoy strengths, respect others, and not depend on wins or praise.

Common mix-up

A common misreading

A common wrong reading: 'Healthy self-esteem means I must feel great about myself all the time.' A better one: it means your worth stays put on bad days too. You can feel sad or embarrassed and still know you matter.

Does it still hold today?

What research says (open when you're curious)

Researchers have argued about this for years. A large review concluded that high self-esteem has not been shown to cause better grades or performance, since the links are modest and hard to untangle from cause and effect (Baumeister et al., 2003). A later review of many studies found that higher self-esteem goes with somewhat better outcomes in relationships, school, work and mental health, but the average effect is small (about .10) (Orth & Robins, 2022). So it helps a little and is not a magic switch.

  • Orth U, Robins RW. Is high self-esteem beneficial? Revisiting a classic question. Am Psychol 2022;77(1):5-17. doi:10.1037/amp0000922 link
  • Baumeister RF, Campbell JD, Krueger JI, Vohs KD. Does high self-esteem cause better performance, interpersonal success, happiness, or healthier lifestyles? Psychol Sci Public Interest 2003;4(1):1-44. link
