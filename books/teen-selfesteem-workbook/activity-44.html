Step 2 · The main line

In one sentence

Core idea

A six-step plan, calm first, then define, brainstorm, choose, test and adjust, makes problems feel manageable and builds confidence.

What the author is getting at

Problems never disappear, but a clear method keeps them from overwhelming you. Practicing the steps builds confidence in handling life's glitches, and that confidence supports healthy self-esteem.

What are the six steps?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
Take a breath and clear your mind; define the problem; brainstorm solutions; choose one and try it; evaluate how it worked; keep it if it worked, or try another from your list.

Why define the problem before solving it?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
You can only change something when you know exactly what needs to change. 'Assignments are complicated' became 'I have trouble understanding exactly what the teacher wants.'

Step 5 · Four angles

Look at it from four sides

What the book says

Problems never disappear, but a clear method keeps them from overwhelming you. Practicing the steps builds confidence in handling life's glitches, and that confidence supports healthy self-esteem.

Common mix-up

A common misreading

'Good problem solvers just know the answer.' They don't; they have a process and keep trying options. And asking for help is part of it. If a problem feels too big or heavy, a trusted adult or counselor can help.

Does it still hold today?

What research says (open when you're curious)

A meta-analysis of 21 samples found problem-solving therapy, which trains problem-solving attitudes and the four skills of defining the problem, generating options, deciding and checking the result, helped reduce depression symptoms about as well as other psychological treatments and medication, and better than no treatment (Bell & D'Zurilla, 2009). It also worked better when it included the whole package. That study is about a clinical program for depression, not a workbook page, so it supports the idea more than this exact six-step list.

  • Bell AC, D'Zurilla TJ. Problem-solving therapy for depression: a meta-analysis. Clin Psychol Rev 2009;29(4):348-353. doi:10.1016/j.cpr.2009.02.003 link

How it connects

Activity 43 (realistic goals) and Activity 41 (positive decisions) fit into steps 3 and 4. Activity 33 (tolerating discomfort) helps with step 1, and Activity 46 (asking for help) is the step to take when a problem is too big alone.
