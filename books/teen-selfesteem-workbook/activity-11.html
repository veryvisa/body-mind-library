Step 2 · The main line

In one sentence

Core idea

Society sends messages about how to look, act, and belong; you can notice them and choose which to follow, as long as your choices stay safe.

What the author is getting at

Part of growing up is finding your own ideas and values. Healthy self-esteem includes staying true to them when they are safe, whether or not society agrees.

What is a social value?
Answer in your head, then tap to flip
An idea a society treats as normal or right, like which hair, jobs, or behavior are seen as good. It reaches you through media, school, community, and people around you.

Does following or resisting society's ideas show what is true for you?

Not by itself. Rebelling just to be different can also be a reaction rather than your own choice. The aim is to find what you really believe.

Step 5 · Four angles

Look at it from four sides

What the book says

Part of growing up is finding your own ideas and values. Healthy self-esteem includes staying true to them when they are safe, whether or not society agrees.

Common mix-up

A common misreading

"Being myself means going against everything society says." Being yourself means choosing consciously. Sometimes you agree with society, and that can be authentic too. If looks or others' teasing weigh on you, if any of this feels heavy, it is okay to talk with a trusted adult or school counselor.

Does it still hold today?

What research says (open when you're curious)

A meta-analysis of 77 studies found that exposure to media showing a very thin ideal body was linked to more body dissatisfaction, more internalizing of that ideal, and more disordered eating beliefs in women, with small to moderate effects (Grabe et al., 2008). It studied women rather than teens specifically, and many studies were correlational, so it supports noticing media messages but not a strong cause.

  • Grabe S, Ward LM, Hyde JS. The role of the media in body image concerns among women: a meta-analysis of experimental and correlational studies. Psychol Bull 2008;134(3). link

How it connects

It completes the family–friends–society run (Activities 9 and 10). The pull of images and trends comes back in Activity 21 (body image facts), Activity 24 (social media), and Activity 7 (diversity).
