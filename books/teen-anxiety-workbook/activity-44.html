Step 2 · The main line第 2 步 · 主线

In one sentence一句话

When frightening public events happen, strong reactions are normal, and small, concrete steps such as talking, limiting news, keeping routines, and having a plan help you feel safer again.可怕的公共事件发生时，反应强烈是正常的；一些具体的小步骤——说出来、少看新闻、保持日常作息、准备好计划——能帮你重新感到安全。

What the author is getting at作者想说的

Stressful public events like disasters, outbreaks, and violence are rare but do happen, and feeling anxious, frightened, or vulnerable afterward is normal; even if you feel powerless to begin with, plenty of steps can help you regain a sense of safety and calm.灾害、疫情、暴力这类令人不安的公共事件虽然少见，但确实会发生；事后感到焦虑、害怕或脆弱是正常的。即使一开始觉得无能为力，你仍有很多办法让自己重新感到安全和平静。

Step 5 · Four angles第 5 步 · 四个角度

Look at it from four sides从四面看它

What the book says书里怎么说

Stressful public events like disasters, outbreaks, and violence are rare but do happen, and feeling anxious, frightened, or vulnerable afterward is normal; even if you feel powerless to begin with, plenty of steps can help you regain a sense of safety and calm.

灾害、疫情、暴力这类令人不安的公共事件虽然少见，但确实会发生；事后感到焦虑、害怕或脆弱是正常的。即使一开始觉得无能为力，你仍有很多办法让自己重新感到安全和平静。

A common misreading一个常见的误读

'If I keep watching the coverage, I'll feel more in control.' It usually works the other way: a little basic information helps, while hours of replays tend to raise stress. Another misreading: 'I'm overreacting because it didn't happen to me.' Reactions to events you only heard about are normal. If, weeks later, you are still having trouble sleeping, concentrating, or feeling safe, that is a good time to talk with a parent, school counselor, or doctor.

「一直看报道，我会更有掌控感。」通常恰恰相反：了解一点基本信息有帮助，而几个小时反复看重播往往让压力升高。另一种误读是「事情没发生在我身上，是我反应过度」。对只是听说的事件有反应，是正常的。如果过了几个星期，你仍然睡不好、难以专心或者没有安全感，那就是和家长、学校辅导员或医生聊聊的好时机。

Does it still hold today?今天还成立吗

What research says (open when you're curious)研究怎么说（想深入再打开）

Research backs the book's advice to limit news and disturbing video. After the 2013 Boston Marathon bombings, a survey of more than 4,600 US adults found that people who spent six or more hours a day on bombing-related media in the following week reported more acute stress than was linked with direct exposure to the bombings themselves (Holman, Garfin & Silver, 2014). It was a survey of adults that shows a link, not proof that cutting back helps, but it is a strong reason to take news in small, factual doses.

研究支持书里「少看新闻和令人不安的视频」的建议。2013 年波士顿马拉松爆炸案之后，一项对 4,600 多名美国成年人的调查发现，事后一周里每天花六小时以上看爆炸相关报道的人，报告的急性压力，比「直接经历爆炸」所关联的还多（Holman、Garfin 与 Silver，2014）。这是一项成人调查，显示的是关联，不能证明少看就一定有帮助；但它是「新闻少量、只看事实」的有力理由。

  • Holman EA, Garfin DR, Silver RC. Media's role in broadcasting acute stress following the Boston Marathon bombings. Proc Natl Acad Sci USA 2014;111(1):93–98. doi:10.1073/pnas.1316265110 link链接
