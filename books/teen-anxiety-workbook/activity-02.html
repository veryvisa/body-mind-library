Step 2 · The main line第 2 步 · 主线

In one sentence一句话

Your brain has an ancient alarm that gets the body ready to fight, run, or freeze; it can't always tell a real danger from a stressful moment, and knowing that lets you calm it on purpose.大脑里有一套古老的警报，会让身体准备好战斗、逃跑或僵住；它常常分不清真危险和只是让人紧张的场面——知道这一点，你就能有意识地让它安静下来。

What the author is getting at作者想说的

Anxiety starts when we believe we are threatened. The amygdala sounds an alarm, hormones prepare the body, and we are ready to fight, flee, or freeze. Most of the time the 'threat' is something we only believe is dangerous, and understanding this lets us choose to calm the body.焦虑始于「我们相信自己受到了威胁」。杏仁核拉响警报，激素让身体就位，我们准备战斗、逃跑或僵住。大多数时候，所谓威胁只是我们以为危险的东西；看懂这一点，就能主动让身体平静。

Step 5 · Four angles第 5 步 · 四个角度

Look at it from four sides从四面看它

What the book says书里怎么说

Anxiety starts when we believe we are threatened. The amygdala sounds an alarm, hormones prepare the body, and we are ready to fight, flee, or freeze. Most of the time the 'threat' is something we only believe is dangerous, and understanding this lets us choose to calm the body.

焦虑始于「我们相信自己受到了威胁」。杏仁核拉响警报，激素让身体就位，我们准备战斗、逃跑或僵住。大多数时候，所谓威胁只是我们以为危险的东西；看懂这一点，就能主动让身体平静。

A common misreading一个常见的误读

'My body is overreacting, so something is broken.' The alarm is working as designed — it is just tuned to be cautious. The goal is not to remove it but to help it tell real danger from discomfort.

「身体反应过度，说明我坏掉了。」警报是在按设计工作，只是调得偏谨慎。目标不是拆掉它，而是帮它分清真危险和不舒服。

Does it still hold today?今天还成立吗

What research says (open when you're curious)研究怎么说（想深入再打开）

Neuroscientist Joseph LeDoux and psychiatrist Daniel Pine propose separating two things: the brain circuits that detect threats and set off the body's responses, and the circuits behind the conscious feeling of fear or anxiety (LeDoux & Pine, 2016). So the amygdala is better pictured as part of an alarm system than as the place where fear is felt. Therapist Pete Walker added a fourth response, 'fawn' (trying to please or appease), to fight, flight, and freeze; it is a clinical idea from trauma therapy rather than a research-established category. The book's simple alarm picture is still a useful teaching model.

神经科学家约瑟夫·勒杜和精神科医生丹尼尔·派恩主张把两件事分开：侦测威胁、启动身体反应的脑回路，和产生有意识的害怕、焦虑感受的回路（LeDoux 与 Pine，2016）。所以杏仁核更像警报系统的一部分，而不是「感到害怕」的地方。治疗师皮特·沃克在战斗、逃跑、僵住之外加了第四种反应「讨好」（想办法取悦、安抚对方）；这是创伤治疗里的临床说法，不是研究确立的分类。书里简化的警报图仍是好用的教学模型。

  • LeDoux JE, Pine DS. Using neuroscience to help understand fear and anxiety: a two-system framework. Am J Psychiatry 2016;173(11):1083–1093. doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2016.16030353 link链接
  • Walker P. The 4Fs: a trauma typology in complex PTSD (author's website). link链接

How it connects和别处怎么连

Every calming tool later in the book — progressive relaxation (25), breathing (30), grounding (28), managing panic (41) — is a way of telling this alarm 'stand down'. Activity 13 ('What's the worst that could happen?') works on the 'is it real danger?' question from the thinking side.

书里后面每个让人平静的工具——渐进式放松（25）、呼吸（30）、着陆技巧（28）、应对惊恐（41）——都是在告诉这套警报「可以解除了」。第 13 个练习「最坏会怎样」则从想法这一侧回答「是真危险吗」。

Practise with the book配合原书练习

Workbook pp. 6–8原书第 6–8 页

Pages 6–8: picture yourself at your door in the morning and list what in an ordinary day might set off your alarm; rank them; then mark which are real dangers and which are 'felt' ones, and which make you want to fight, run, or hide. The book adds an important note on page 8: if anything on the list is a real danger to you, talk to an adult you trust about getting help.原书第 6–8 页：想象早上站在房门口，列出普通一天里可能触发警报的事；排个序；再标出哪些是真危险、哪些是「感觉上的」危险，哪些让你想反击、想逃、想躲。第 8 页有一句重要提醒：如果清单上有对你真正的危险，去找一位信任的大人帮忙。
